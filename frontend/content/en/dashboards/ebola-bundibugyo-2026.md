### Why this outbreak is unusual

Most of the ebolavirus epidemics known from the media (West Africa 2014–16, DRC 2018–20) were caused by **Ebola virus** (EBOV), for which approved vaccines (Ervebo, Zabdeno+Mvabea) and treatment (atoltivimab/maftivimab/odesivimab) exist.

This 2026 outbreak is caused by **Bundibugyo virus** (BDBV) — a different ebolavirus species with important clinical and epidemiological differences:

| | Ebola virus (EBOV) | Bundibugyo virus (BDBV) |
|---|---|---|
| Case fatality | ~50–90% | ~25–36% (lower) |
| Approved vaccine | ✅ Ervebo, Zabdeno | ❌ None approved |
| Approved treatment | ✅ Inmazeb | ❌ None approved |
| Previous epidemics | DRC 1976, 2018–20… | DRC/Uganda 2007, Uganda 2012 |

### Genomics

- The virus was sequenced soon after detection — the first 3 genomes were published on 18 May 2026
- Database: **[Pathoplexus/LAPIS](https://pathoplexus.org)** — 185 records, 141 assigned to BDBV-2026
- Phylogenetic analysis confirmed a relationship to strains from the 2007 Uganda epidemic

### Treatment and vaccine research

Clinical trials currently under way for BDBV:

- **PANTHER** — antiviral trial (remdesivir and others)
- **MBP134** — bispecific antibody (announced June 2026)
- Remdesivir — repurposing trial

Detailed overview: [Treatment and vaccines](https://titan.img.cas.cz/ebola/lecba-vakciny.html)

### Risk to the Czech Republic

BDBV is not endemic in the Czech Republic or in Europe. An imported case (as in France) is possible among:
- Humanitarian workers in the DRC or Uganda
- Healthcare staff returning from affected areas

The National Institute of Public Health and Bulovka University Hospital maintain active preparedness for imported VHF (viral haemorrhagic fevers).
Details: [The Czech Republic and Ebola](https://titan.img.cas.cz/ebola/cesko.html)
