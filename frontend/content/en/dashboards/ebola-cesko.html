In its 23 October assessment, ECDC considers the overall risk to the general EU/EEA population very low, and the risk to people living in or travelling to affected areas of Kenya and Nigeria low if preventive measures are followed. It does not recommend travel restrictions for Kenya and Nigeria. Following the controlled evacuation on 26 October, HUG reports that the Swiss FOPH considers the likelihood of transmission in the general Swiss population very low. ECDC; HUG.

On 20 October, Nigeria’s health ministry and WHO confirmed one imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease in Abuja. NCDC reports 53 contacts and no deaths as of 24 October; another suspected case in Rivers State tested negative. On 26 October, the patient was medically evacuated under controlled conditions to HUG in Geneva, where she is stable and in isolation. Her husband is a contact monitored in a special isolation unit at USZ in Zurich, not a second confirmed case. Where the patient acquired the infection remains under investigation. NCDC; HUG, 26 October.

Uganda’s health minister stated on 25 October that no further case had been confirmed since the outbreak ended. All 60 contacts of the Kenyan patient in Uganda completed 21-day follow-up without symptoms. The statement confirms that movement across the DRC border is limited to essential travel, returning Ugandan citizens face 21 days of quarantine at their own expense, and travellers with a history of travel to the DRC may not transit through Uganda. Contacts of an active case may not fly out before completing follow-up. Check requirements before travelling. Uganda Media Centre / Ministry of Health.

CDC issued a new order on 22 October: from 3 November 2026 at 11:59 p.m. EST, passengers boarding an international flight to the United States, including connections through the US, must attest that they have not been in the DRC during the preceding 21 days. Transit through a DRC airport is included; government exemptions apply. This new requirement is not yet in force on 27 October. It is a US measure, not a Czech or EU rule. CDC.

Practical bottom line for the Czech Republic: the ordinary risk to the public is very low. Laboratory testing ruled out Ebola disease and other highly dangerous infections in a woman with fever after her return from Uganda on 2 August. Transfer under controlled conditions and precautionary isolation at Bulovka did not represent a confirmed case or community transmission.

On 19 October, CDC issued a Level 1 travel health notice for Kenya: practise usual precautions and avoid contact with people with symptoms and their body fluids. This is not a travel ban on Kenya. CDC: Kenya.

Bulovka and Těchonín

Bulovka

Bulovka University Hospital operates the National Centre for the Isolation and Treatment of Highly Dangerous Infections. The Czech authorities used it in May for precautionary monitoring of a US physician after a risk contact and on 1 August for a woman with fever after returning from Uganda. Transfer under controlled conditions and an isolation regime do not mean the uncontrolled introduction of infection. The US physician did not develop the disease, and Ebola was ruled out in the woman by laboratory testing on 2 August.

Těchonín

The Těchonín Centre for Biological Protection is a specialised military facility for biological protection. According to iROZHLAS/ČTK, a Czech soldier was precautionarily isolated there after returning from a UN mission in the DRC. When communicating about this, it is best to give 22 May 2026 as the date of the event and 2 June 2026 as the date of publication.

For the public: the Czech precautionary isolations are important for preparedness and communication, but on their own they do not change the risk assessment for the general public.

What is the risk for the Czech Republic

For the public

The risk to the public in the Czech Republic remains very low. The Czech Republic is not in the vicinity of the affected states, and Ebola does not spread through the air like influenza or COVID-19. In its 21 September press release, ECDC continues to assess the risk of importation into the EU/EEA as very low; neither the laboratory-negative Czech suspected case nor the controlled medical evacuation to Frankfurt of a patient infected in the DRC changes that assessment.

On 20 October, Nigeria’s health ministry and WHO confirmed one imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease in Abuja. NCDC reports 53 contacts and no deaths as of 24 October; another suspected case in Rivers State tested negative. On 26 October, the patient was medically evacuated under controlled conditions to HUG in Geneva, where she is stable and in isolation. Her husband is a contact monitored in a special isolation unit at USZ in Zurich, not a second confirmed case. Where the patient acquired the infection remains under investigation. NCDC; HUG, 26 October.

According to WHO AFRO, Kenya has recorded nine confirmed cases and two deaths as of 26 October. All eight secondary cases were detected among contacts already in quarantine; there is no evidence of wider community transmission. Twenty contacts completed quarantine after 21 days without symptoms, while 98 remain in quarantine. WHO AFRO, 26 October.

For travellers

Heightened attention applies to people who in the last 21 days were in the affected areas of the DRC, especially in Ituri, North Kivu, South Kivu, Haut-Uele, Tshopo, Bas-Uele, Sud-Ubangi or Nord-Ubangi, or in Uganda, worked in healthcare in the outbreak zone, attended a funeral, cared for a sick person, or had contact with the bodily fluids of a person with suspected or confirmed Ebola disease. ECDC distinguishes an ordinary return without exposure from low-risk and high-risk exposure; measures should be guided by the specific type of contact, not merely by presence in the country.

Return from an affected area and exposure

On 15 June ECDC recommends, for people without exposure, mainly clear information about symptoms and what to do if problems arise. For low-risk exposure it recommends 21 days of self-monitoring of health status, that is, measuring temperature and watching for symptoms twice a day. For high-risk exposure it recommends active monitoring for 21 days, being reachable by public health services, not travelling abroad and, depending on circumstances, limiting social contacts or clinical work.

ECDC reaffirmed very low risk to the general public on 1 October following the medical evacuation to the Netherlands. ECDC.

The ECDC model described on the page checked on 27 October covers the general population of Ituri and North Kivu. It excludes returning health workers and other professionals, who have greater exposure risk and are also subject to specific control measures and monitoring.

The ECDC model for the period 11–25 June and the follow-up preparedness overview, published on 18 June and updated on 10 July, estimate the risk of importation into the EU/EEA as low; in the overview ECDC expresses it as roughly one importation per 24,000 travellers from the main outbreak area into the EU/EEA, with a wide interval of uncertainty. The imported case in France shows that the risk is not zero, but it does not change the ECDC conclusion that the ordinary risk to the public in the Czech Republic remains very low and that the key factor is the rapid identification of symptomatic people with a relevant travel or exposure history.

WHO's updated temporary recommendations of 24 August advise other states to provide current information to travellers, including advice against travel to areas with community transmission. WHO does not recommend flight suspensions or denial of entry to travellers. This is not a blanket travel ban; Czech advice should be read according to the specific destination and exposure.

On 1 October, the Czech MFA updated its DRC warning and continued to advise against travel and urged Czech citizens to leave promptly. It lists the seven provinces affected at that time and reports that the DRC Ministry of Health replaced the earlier blanket quarantine with individual measures based on exposure in categories A, B and C. This is the Czech MFA’s account of DRC rules, not ECDC categories. Specific travel conditions should be checked in the current advice. MZV ČR.

On 16 June the Ugandan Ministry of Health also announced that travellers leaving Uganda do not need a so-called “Ebola-Free Certificate”; testing should be guided by symptoms, contact with a confirmed case and the assessment of health authorities.

On 10 October, RIVM announced that the patient treated at LUMC in Leiden had recovered, tested negative and left hospital on 9 October after isolation ended. RIVM’s 12 October update continues to assess the likelihood of spread in the Netherlands and Europe as very small. RIVM.

US travel measures need to be read separately: CDC’s situation summary was checked on 27 October, and the returning-travellers page was updated on 11 September. CDC says that travellers, including US citizens, who were in the DRC within 21 days before their flight may not board commercial flights to the United States; US citizens and nationals who were only in Uganda or South Sudan must enter through designated airports for enhanced screening. On 11 October, CDC issued a further order extending restrictions for specified foreign nationals for 30 days. CDC recommends avoiding all travel to Ituri and North Kivu, avoiding nonessential travel to Haut-Uele and Tshopo, and taking enhanced precautions in other parts of the DRC and in Uganda. These are US rules, not Czech or EU measures, and they do not change the ECDC conclusion of very low risk for the general EU/EEA population.

For health workers

Suspected Ebola is rare in the Czech setting, but it requires a procedure rehearsed in advance: telephone triage, travel history, isolation, protective equipment, contact with the public health service and coordination with a specialised infectious-diseases unit.

If in the last 21 days you have returned from an area of the DRC or Uganda with documented Bundibugyo virus cases and you develop a fever, marked weakness, vomiting, diarrhoea or bleeding symptoms, contact a health service by telephone and state your travel history. Do not go to a waiting room without arranging it first.

On 8 October, the Czech MFA updated its Uganda advice: travel to areas bordering the DRC only when strictly necessary and exercise increased caution elsewhere. According to the MFA, the Uganda–DRC border remains closed to ordinary movement, and Ugandan citizens and residents returning from the DRC face mandatory quarantine. The end of Uganda’s outbreak does not remove the risk of reintroduction. Check the requirements before travelling. Czech MFA.
