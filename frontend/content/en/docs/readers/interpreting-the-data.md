## Reported cases are not true occurrence

Most dashboards show **reported cases** or **laboratory detections**. Their number depends not only
on how many people fell ill, but also on how much testing is done, how many of the ill see a doctor
and how consistently cases are reported. A rise in reported cases may therefore mean a real rise in
illness, but also more testing or a change in reporting.

## Counts and positivity

The number of laboratory detections grows with the number of specimens tested. To compare periods
with different testing volumes, **positivity** — the share of positives among the specimens tested —
is more suitable. It too depends on who is tested.

## Small numbers

For rare diseases and in small regions the counts are small, and differences between years or
regions may be random. One extra case can mean a large percentage increase on a small number.

## Comparing regions

Absolute counts mostly reflect the size of a region's population. Compare regions by **incidence per
100,000 population**. Even that need not reflect a true difference in occurrence: regions may differ
in access to testing and in how consistently they report. The rates on the portal are not
age-standardised, so a region with an older population may show higher values for diseases that
mainly affect older people.

## Breaks in series

A change in definition, in how cases are reported or in the number of reporting sites creates a jump
in a time series that does not reflect a change in occurrence. An example is the new reporting
channel in ISIN from July 2025 (see [Signals](/en/signals/)). Known breaks are listed in the chart
notes and in the [source catalogue](/en/about/data/).

## The original source is authoritative

The portal takes over and processes data from public institutions. If a value on the portal differs
from the source, the source's value applies. Common reasons for differences are given in the
[FAQ](/en/docs/readers/faq/).
